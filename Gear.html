Gear

Know what to carry, what to wear, and what to trust — gear explained by system, rated by use, and matched to the terrain.

1. Navigation

Map, compass, GPS device/app, altimeter

2. Illumination

Headlamp, spare batteries

3. Sun Protection

4. First Aid

A custom first-aid kit

Matches, lighter, fire starter

Knife, duct tape, multi-tool

8. Nutrition

Extra food

9. Hydration

Water bottles/bladder, purification

10. Emergency Shelter

Bivy sack, emergency blanket, tarp

Why You Should Gear Up

In the mountains, gear is not accessories — it is your margin for error. Weather turns faster than forecasts predict, a route takes longer than planned, and a single failure — a blown zipper, a broken strap, a dull crampon point — can escalate a manageable situation into a dangerous one. Proper gear does three things: it keeps you dry and warm when your body can no longer do it alone, it gives you traction and protection on terrain that punishes mistakes, and it buys you time when plans fall apart. The right kit won't make a climb easy, and it won't compensate for poor judgment — but the wrong kit can end a trip, or worse. That's why experienced mountaineers spend years refining their systems rather than chasing the newest item: they're not collecting equipment, they're building reliability.

Schools

Everything you need to know about mountaineering schools, in one place.

Himalayan Mountaineering Institute
Darjeeling, India

Founded in 1954, shortly after the first ascent of Everest, with Tenzing Norgay as its first Director of Field Training. It is one of the best-known climbing schools in Asia.

Nehru Institute of Mountaineering
Uttarkashi, India

Established in 1965 in the Garhwal Himalaya, it is known for training climbers and mountaineering instructors in the region.

Compagnie des Guides de Chamonix
Chamonix, France

Founded in 1821 and often described as the world's oldest company of mountain guides, based in the birthplace of alpinism.

ENSA
Chamonix, France

The École Nationale de Ski et d'Alpinisme is France's national school for training mountain guides and ski instructors.

Plas y Brenin
Snowdonia, Wales, UK

The UK's National Mountain Sports Centre, known for mountaineering, climbing and outdoor training in the mountains of North Wales.

American Alpine Institute
Washington, USA

Founded in 1966, it is a long-established school known for teaching climbing and mountaineering, from basic skills to expedition-level objectives.

NOLS
Wyoming, USA

The National Outdoor Leadership School, founded in 1965, is known for teaching wilderness skills, leadership and backcountry travel.

Why Mountaineering Schools are Important

Mountaineering is one of the few sports where a small mistake - a misread weather window, a badly placed anchor, an unroped traverse - can be fatal. Mountains don't offer second attempts, and experience alone is a slow and dangerous teacher. That's why mountaineering schools exist: to compress years of hard-won knowledge into structured, supervised training before a climber ever faces those consequences on a real objective.

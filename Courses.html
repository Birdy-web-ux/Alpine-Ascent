Foundational & Government-Run Courses (India)

These are highly structured, long-form courses run by government institutes
and are a well-known gateway for those in the Indian subcontinent.

Basic Mountaineering Course (BMC)

  • Provider/Location: Run by several government institutes in India, including NIM (Uttarkashi), HMI (Darjeeling), and ABVIMAS (Manali)
  • Duration: 26-28 days, residential
  • Difficulty: Beginner. Requires a good level of physical fitness but no prior technical experience. The course covers rock, ice, and snow craft, finishing with a graduation climb on a peak of around 4,400-4,800m.
  • Indicative Cost: Fees vary by institute. HMI lists a subsidized fee of ₹24,802 for Indians and US $1,289 for foreigners. Other sources cite a range of roughly ₹15,000-₹17,000.

Advanced Mountaineering Course (AMC)

  • Provider/Location: Same institutes as the BMC (NIM, HMI, etc.)
  • Duration: 28 days.
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to Advanced. This is the next step after a BMC and typically requires an 'A' grade in the basic course to enroll. It qualifies climbers for peaks up to 7,000m.
  • Indicative Cost: At HMI, the AMC fee is the same as the BMC: ₹24,802 / US $1,289.

Commercial Guiding & Skills Courses (New Zealand)

Alpine Guides (Aoraki Mount Cook) offers a suite of courses that are very well-regarded
internationally for their progression and quality of instruction.

Mountain Skills Course (MSC)

  • Location: Aoraki Mount Cook, New Zealand.
  • Duration: 3.5 days.
  • Difficulty: Introductory. Designed for hikers and hunters to learn essential winter skills like crampon and ice axe use, avalanche awareness, and safe snow travel. No prior mountaineering experience is required.
  • Indicative Cost: NZ $1,775 - $2,675 per person, depending on group size. A 1:1 guide ratio is NZ $4,375.

Alpine Skills Course (ASC)

  • Location: Aoraki Mount Cook, New Zealand.
  • Duration: 7 days.
  • Difficulty: Introductory to Intermediate. This course introduces climbing on snow and ice and serves as a prerequisite for their advanced course.
  • Indicative Cost: NZ $4,695 - $4,875 per person.

Technical Mountaineering Course (TMC)

  • Location: Aoraki Mount Cook, New Zealand.
  • Duration: 8 days.
  • Difficulty: Advanced. This is Alpine Guides' highest-level scheduled course. It requires prior experience (like their ASC or equivalent) and focuses on leading, complex ropework, and climbing on steep ice and mixed terrain. Prerequisites include confidence on 40°+ ice and multi-pitch rock skills.
  • Indicative Cost: NZ $1,775 - $2,675 per person, depending on group size. A 1:1 ratio is NZ $4,375.

Long-Format & Expedition-Style Courses

These are immersive programs, often run by non-profits or large schools, that combine skills
training with extended time in the mountains.

The Mountaineers Alpine Scrambling Course

  • Location: Washington State, USA (Cascade and Olympic Mountains).
  • Duration: 12 weeks (a comprehensive program with lectures and field trips).
  • Difficulty: Beginner to Intermediate. Focuses on non-roped travel to reach summits, covering off-trail navigation, snow travel, and self-arrest. It does not cover glacier travel or roped climbing.
  • Indicative Cost: $450 for members, $540 for guests. Note that separate fees for required Wilderness First Aid and Navigation courses are not included.

NOLS (National Outdoor Leadership School) Mountaineering

  • Location: Various, including Alaska, Patagonia (Chile), and the North Cascades (USA).
  • Duration: Varies widely, from 2-week skills courses to semester-length (68–80 days) expeditions.
  • Difficulty: Beginner to Advanced, depending on the specific course. NOLS is known for its immersive, expedition-based approach to teaching leadership and technical skills.
  • Indicative Cost: A 2-week Alaska Mountaineering course is ~$6,650, a 68-day Summer Semester in the Rockies is ~$14,940, and a 4-week youth course is listed at $3,000.

The Professional Standard (IFMGA)

This is the pinnacle of mountaineering instruction, often called the
"Ph.D. of mountaineering".

IFMGA Mountain Guide Certification

  • Provider/Location: Administered by member associations in over 20 countries (e.g., AMGA in the USA, NZMGA in New Zealand).
  • Duration: The full training and assessment process takes at least five years to complete.
  • Difficulty: Professional / Expert. This is a multi-stage, multi-year program. Candidates must already be highly experienced climbers. The training involves rigorous courses and exams in rock, alpine, and ski guiding, including a 21-day final test guiding a client.
  • Indicative Cost: The total cost to attain certification is often cited as around $30,000 or more, spread across the various courses and exams over several years.

!IMPORTANT NOTICE!

Beyond the programs above, other excellent schools offer similar courses. For example, Jagged Globe in the European Alps runs a progression of courses from an Alpine Introduction (£1,720, 6 days) to an advanced Alpine Climber course (£2,795, 6 days). The UIAA also runs youth camps and training events, often at a lower cost (e.g., a 9-day camp in Chamonix for €600) to make the sport more accessible.
